Cathode Caster
Part Cathode Ray Tube television, part guitar.

It's no secret that I'm a huge fan of old tech. Old cameras, iPods, audio equipment, computers, all of it. Perhaps one of my favorite pieces of old tech is the Cathode Ray Tube television. The looks of the display, the rich range of colors, the aesthetic of a VHS being played on one, the high level of serviceability, and that sweet sixty hertz hum all make CRTs what they are. Needless to say, when I saw the incredible work of ELECTRONICOS FANTASTICOS! and their CRTelecaster, I knew I had to make one of my own. Having little knowledge of electronics, I sat on the idea for a few years until stumbling across a pair of JVC TM-9U monitors. The first one I was able to turn into an oscillograph for live visuals, which was a surprisingly easy conversion and a huge crowd-pleaser.

After sourcing a few guitar parts from a friend, and buying plenty of tools and supplies, I got busy on making my own version of the CRTelecaster. For the most part, everything went smoothly! The code came together well, the neck and body assembly worked nicely, I was getting signals from the TV with my pickup, and I finally learned the difference between a Raspberry Pi and an Arduino, since I ran into the issue of needing an analog input on the RPi (the MCP3008 is a great ADC, by the way). It can currently play sine, square, saw, and triangle waves, and it can switch between 4 different octaves and various combinations of strings using buttons on the breadboard. You can see the BOM, construction process, and code in greater detail over on my GitHub repo.
